How to Run Ads That Actually Work
for a Local Business

Ads work for a local business when five things line up: a clear offer, the right platform, creative that stops the scroll, a landing page built for that one ad, and tracking that tells you what each enquiry cost.

Published 23 December 2026 / by Lokesh Barani Kumar

Ads work for a local business when five things line up: a clear offer, the right platform for how your customers buy, creative that stops the scroll, a landing page built for that one ad, and tracking that tells you what each enquiry cost. Get one wrong and you pay for clicks that go nowhere. Get all five right and ads become a tap you can turn up when you want more work.

This guide is written for owners spending their own money, usually a few hundred to a few thousand dollars a month, not big brands with agency budgets.

Quick facts

  • Ads amplify what already works. Fix your Google profile, reviews and website first, or you'll pay to send people somewhere that doesn't convert.
  • Google Ads catches people already searching for you. Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) reach people who aren't searching yet.
  • The number that matters is cost per paying customer, not cost per click.
01

Before you spend a dollar: are you ready for ads?

If you can't tick at least four of these, spend the next month on the basics in 10 ways to make your business stand out instead.

  • Your Google Business Profile is complete and has recent reviews
  • Your website loads fast on a phone and has one clear call to action
  • You reply to enquiries within a few hours
  • You know roughly what a new customer is worth to you
  • You can track where enquiries come from

03

Set a budget you can learn from

Work backwards from what a customer is worth. If a new client spends $600 with you and you'd happily pay $100 to win one, that's your target cost per customer. If roughly one in four enquiries becomes a customer, you can afford about $25 per enquiry.

Then give the test enough room to tell you something. A campaign that gets a handful of clicks a week won't show you what works. Pick a monthly amount you're comfortable treating as the cost of learning, commit to it for at least four weeks, and decide in advance what result would make you keep going. There's a worked example in how much a small business should spend on ads.

04

Lead with a real offer

An offer gives people a reason to act now rather than "sometime". It doesn't have to be a discount.

  • A free first consultation, quote or inspection
  • A package price that's easier to understand than hourly rates
  • A guarantee, such as a free re-do if they're not happy
  • Something time-bound and true, like limited spots before the school holidays

Whatever you choose, it has to be genuine. Under Australian Consumer Law, fake urgency, made-up "was" prices and misleading claims can get you into real trouble.

05

Google Ads vs Facebook Ads: which is better for a local business?

Google Ads is usually better for local service businesses whose customers search when they have a need, like plumbers, driving schools and accountants. Facebook and Instagram ads usually work better for businesses that need to create demand or show something visual, like food, events, products and experiences. Many businesses end up using both for different jobs.

Google's Local Services Ads may also be worth checking if they're offered for your category and area. Full comparison in Google Ads vs Meta Ads for a local service business.

Google Ads tips for local businesses

  • Target a tight radius around where you actually work
  • Use specific keywords ("driving lessons Marion") rather than broad ones ("driving")
  • Add negative keywords to block searches like "jobs", "free" and "DIY"
  • Run ads during the hours you can answer the phone
  • Use call assets so people can ring you straight from the ad

Meta ads tips for local businesses

  • Use video wherever you can, and keep it short and vertical
  • Start with a local radius and let Meta's targeting do more of the work than detailed interest lists
  • Run two or three different creatives, not one
  • Look at the Meta Ad Library to see what competitors are running, then do something different
06

How do you make ad creative that converts?

Ad creative converts when it hooks attention in the first second, shows a real person or real result, speaks to one specific problem, and gives one clear next step. Polished production matters much less than relevance. A phone video of you explaining something useful usually beats a glossy brand ad for a local business.

A simple structure for a 15 to 30 second video ad:

  1. Hook (first 1 to 2 seconds): name the problem or say something surprising
  2. Proof (next 5 to 10 seconds): show the work, the result, or a customer
  3. Offer (next 5 seconds): what they get and why now
  4. Action (last 3 seconds): exactly what to tap

Always add captions. Many people watch with the sound off. For ready-made openings, see 10 ad hooks that work for local businesses.

Test properly

Change one thing at a time. Run two versions with different hooks and the same everything else. After a week or two, keep the winner and test a new challenger against it. Over a few months, this compounds.

07

Ad landing page best practices

The best landing page for an ad is a page built for that ad alone: the same message and offer the person just clicked, one clear action, proof right beside it, and nothing else to wander off to. Sending ad traffic to your home page is one of the most expensive habits in small business marketing.

  • A headline that matches the ad. If the ad says "Free roof inspection in Canberra", so does the page.
  • One action. A short form, a call button or a booking link. Not all three competing.
  • Proof next to the action. Reviews, star rating, licence numbers, before and after photos.
  • Fast loading on a phone. Most ad clicks come from mobile. A slow page wastes paid clicks.
  • No main navigation menu, so visitors stay focused on the one thing.
  • Short answers to common objections: price, timing, service area.
08

Track what each customer actually costs

If you can't see which ads led to enquiries, you're guessing. Set up conversion tracking before the first ad goes live:

  1. Install Google Analytics 4 and mark form submissions, calls and bookings as key events
  2. Connect conversion tracking in Google Ads and the Meta pixel or Conversions API
  3. Use UTM tags on every ad link so you can see the source in your analytics
  4. Ask every new customer how they found you, and write it down
  5. If you show a cookie banner, make sure your tracking respects people's choices

Check once a week: spend, enquiries, cost per enquiry, and how many became customers. Step-by-step setup in how to tell if your ads are working.

09

Frequently asked questions

What is the best advertising for a small local business?

For most local service businesses, Google Ads targeting local search terms is the best starting point, because it reaches people who already need you. For visual or impulse businesses like food, retail and events, Instagram and Facebook ads often work better.

How much should a small business spend on ads?

Work backwards from what a customer is worth and what you can afford to spend finding one. Commit to a test budget you're comfortable losing for at least four weeks, so you get enough data to make a decision.

Why are my ads getting clicks but no enquiries?

Usually it's the landing page or the offer, not the ad. Check that the page matches the ad, loads fast on mobile, has one clear action and shows proof. Also check that your conversion tracking is working.

Should I run ads myself or hire someone?

You can run a small Google or Meta campaign yourself if you're willing to learn and check it weekly. Hire help when your spend grows, when results stall, or when your time is worth more doing the actual work.

How long until ads start working?

You'll see clicks within days, but give a campaign about four weeks of steady spend before judging it. The ad platforms need time to learn who responds, and you need enough enquiries to see a pattern.
