AEO (answer engine optimisation) means making your content easy for search engines and voice
assistants to pull out as a direct answer. GEO (generative engine optimisation) means making your
business easy for AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews to
understand, trust and recommend. Both build on good SEO. If your customers ask AI tools for
recommendations, and more of them do every year, you need them.

How AI tools decide who to recommend
AI tools build answers from content they can read and trust. When someone asks "best driving
school in Adelaide" or "who can fix my hot water system in Canberra", the tool looks for businesses
with clear, consistent information across several sources. That includes your website, your Google
profile, reviews and directory listings.
If your website is vague, blocks AI crawlers, or lists a different phone number from your Google
profile, you're harder to recommend with confidence.

7 things to do first
1. Answer real questions on your website. Put the direct answer in the first sentence, then the
   detail.

How to check whether AI tools mention you
Once a month, ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude the questions your customers would
ask, like "who's a good [your service] in [your suburb]?". Note whether you're mentioned and
whether the details are correct. If they get something wrong, fix the source: usually your website or
Google profile.

Frequently asked questions
Is SEO dead because of AI?
No. AEO and GEO build on the same foundations as SEO: useful content, a fast and technically
sound site, and trust. Good SEO makes AEO and GEO much easier.
Can I pay to appear in ChatGPT answers?
Not in the normal answers. Being mentioned comes from having clear, trustworthy information
about your business across the web.
What is an llms.txt file?
A plain-text file at the root of your website that summarises your business and links to your most
important pages, written for AI tools to read. It's a newer, emerging convention, quick to add.
