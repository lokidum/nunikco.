Rams Driving Academy is a local, owner-operated driving school. We rebuilt its website three times

The problem
Word of mouth is a great base for a driving school, but it's invisible to anyone searching "driving
lessons near me". Rams needed to be found on Google, trusted on first look, and easy to contact.

What we did
Google Business Profile
We set up and verified the Google Business Profile, then built it out properly: services, service
areas, photos and a profile image designed for a circular avatar. The school's horizontal logo would
have cropped badly, so we made a square icon version.
Every review gets a personal reply written in the owner's own voice, not a template. New profiles
go through a trust period with Google, so we kept the flow of reviews natural rather than chasing a
sudden spike.

Website, three rounds
We didn't get it perfect the first time, and that's part of the story. Each rebuild fixed what we
learned from the last one:
    A memorable design. A scroll-driven road that draws itself down the page as visitors scroll,
    with smooth animations, so the site feels like a driving school rather than a template.

What other local businesses can take from this
1. Reviews compound. Each new review makes the next customer more likely to call. Replying
   personally shows future customers there's a real person behind the business.
2. Your first website doesn't have to be your last. Launch, learn what customers respond to, and
   improve.
3. Small technical details matter. Schema, suburb lists and AI crawler access take a day to set up
   and keep paying off.
4. Measure paying customers, not just traffic. 300 clicks is a good number. 5 new students a
   month is the one that pays the bills.

Frequently asked questions
How do driving instructors get more students online?
Start with a complete Google Business Profile and a steady flow of reviews, then a fast website that
lists your suburbs, prices and services clearly and makes booking easy.
Do suburb pages help local SEO?
Yes, when each one is useful and specific. Listing the suburbs you serve helps Google match you
to local searches. Avoid copy-paste pages that only swap the suburb name.
Should a small business reply to every Google review?
Yes. Replies show future customers you care, and a personal reply in the owner's voice reads far
better than a template. Reply to negative reviews calmly and offer to sort it out offline.
