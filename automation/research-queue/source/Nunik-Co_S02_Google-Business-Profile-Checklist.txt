      Google Business Profile Checklist for
      Local Businesses
      Setup, categories, photos, posts and reviews, plus the mistakes that hold profiles
      back. Tick through it in an afternoon.
      By Lokesh Barani Kumar, founder of Nunik Co. · November 2026



A strong Google Business Profile has every field filled in, the right primary category, accurate hours
and service areas, real photos, regular posts, and a steady flow of reviews with replies. For local
businesses, it's often the first thing customers see on Google, before they ever reach your website.
Use this checklist to set yours up or audit it.

Ownership and verification
    ☐ You (or your business) hold owner access, not a former marketer, web designer or family
    member
    ☐ The profile is verified
    ☐ At least one backup person has manager access
    ☐ No duplicate profiles exist for your business (search your name and phone number to check)

Ownership comes first for a reason. We've had a client launch held up because a third party still
held admin access to their profile.

Core details
    ☐ Business name exactly as it appears on your signage, with no extra keywords stuffed in
    ☐ The most specific primary category that fits ("Driving school", not "School")
    ☐ Secondary categories for other genuine services
    ☐ Address, or service areas if you travel to customers
    ☐ Phone number that matches your website exactly
    ☐ Website link, ideally with tracking so you can see visits from your profile
    ☐ Regular hours, plus special hours for public holidays
    ☐ Business description that says what you do, who for and where, in plain words


Services and products
    ☐ Every service listed, with a short description
    ☐ Prices or starting prices where you can share them
    ☐ Products added if you sell physical items

    ☐ Attributes ticked where they apply (wheelchair accessible, online appointments and so on)


Photos
    ☐ Logo, designed to work as a square or circle (wide logos crop badly)
    ☐ Cover photo
    ☐ Real photos of your team, your work, your vehicle or premises
    ☐ New photos added every month or two
    ☐ No stock photos


Reviews
    ☐ A simple way to send customers your review link
    ☐ A habit of asking every happy customer
    ☐ A reply to every review, positive or negative, in your own voice
    ☐ Never offer rewards for reviews, and never ask only the customers you think will be happy

Rams Driving Academy, whose owner replies to reviews personally, has passed 100 Google
reviews. Read the case study.

Ongoing activity
    ☐ A post every week or two: an offer, a tip, a recent job, an update
    ☐ Questions in the Q&A section answered
    ☐ Messages switched on only if you'll reply quickly
    ☐ Details checked every few months and after any change


Common mistakes that hold profiles back
1. Keyword stuffing the business name, which can get your profile suspended
2. Choosing a broad category instead of the specific one
3. Different phone numbers or addresses across your profile, website and other listings
4. A logo that crops badly into a circle
5. Ignoring reviews, especially negative ones
6. Leaving the profile untouched for months
