      10 Ad Hooks That Work for Local
      Businesses
      Ten hook formulas with ready-to-use examples for trades, driving schools, food and
      services. Stop the scroll in two seconds.
      By Lokesh Barani Kumar, founder of Nunik Co. · November 2026



An ad hook is the first line or first two seconds of your ad, the part that decides whether someone
keeps watching or scrolls past. The hooks that work best for local businesses name a specific
problem, mention the local area, show a real result, or say something surprising. A strong hook
matters more than polished production.

The 10 hooks
 #     Hook                             Example
 1     Call out the local area          "Gungahlin parents, this one's for you."
 2     Name the exact problem           "Hot water gone cold again?"

 3     The common mistake               "Most learners fail their test on this one thing."
 4     The before and after             Show the clean gutter, the finished kitchen, the passed test first. Explain
                                        how after.

 5     The honest price                 "Here's exactly what a pest inspection costs in Canberra."
 6     The question they're already     "How many driving lessons do you actually need?"
       asking
 7     The behind the scenes            "Here's what happens to your order after you tap buy."

 8     The customer's words             A real review read aloud, or a real customer on camera (with
                                        permission).

 9     The myth buster                  "No, you don't need to replace the whole roof."

 10    The face and the name            The owner introducing themselves and the business, straight to camera.


Why these work
People stop for their own suburb, and it tells them you're nearby. Naming a problem they have
today keeps them watching. The fear of an avoidable mistake is strong. Price transparency stands
out because so few businesses do it. Answering a question people already search gives them a
reason to stay for the answer. And a real person talking straight to camera builds trust fast,
especially for small businesses.

How to use them
    Write three hooks for the same ad, change nothing else, and test them against each other
    Put the hook in the first one to two seconds, both spoken and as on-screen text
    Add captions, because many people watch with the sound off
    Keep the promise. If the hook mentions price, show the price
    Avoid fake urgency and made-up claims. Australian Consumer Law applies to ads too
Once you have the hook, build the rest of the ad around it: proof, offer, then one clear action. More
in how to run ads that actually work.
