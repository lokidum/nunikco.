      How to Tell If Your Ads Are Working:
      GA4 Basics
      Track whether your Google and Meta ads bring in enquiries, using GA4 key events,
      UTM tags and a simple weekly check.
      By Lokesh Barani Kumar, founder of Nunik Co. · November 2026



To tell if your ads are working, track enquiries, not clicks. Set up Google Analytics 4 (GA4) to
record form submissions, phone calls and bookings as key events, connect those to Google Ads
and Meta, tag every ad link with UTM parameters, and check cost per enquiry and cost per
customer each week. Without this, you're judging ads on guesswork.

What to measure
 Metric                      What it tells you                       Where to find it
 Spend                       What you paid                           Google Ads, Meta Ads Manager
 Enquiries                   Forms, calls and bookings from ads      GA4 key events, ad platform conversions

 Cost per enquiry            Spend divided by enquiries              Work it out weekly
 Customers                   Enquiries that became paying jobs       Your CRM or booking system

 Cost per customer           Spend divided by customers              Work it out monthly

Clicks, impressions and likes are useful for diagnosing problems, but they don't tell you whether
ads are making money.

Step-by-step setup
1. Install GA4 on your website
Create a GA4 property in Google Analytics and add the tag to every page of your site, either
directly or through Google Tag Manager.

2. Track the actions that matter
Set up events for form submissions (ideally fired when the thank-you message appears), clicks on
your phone number, clicks on WhatsApp or booking buttons, and completed bookings or
purchases. Then mark them as key events in GA4, which is GA4's name for the actions you care
most about.

3. Connect Google Ads
Link Google Ads to GA4 and import your key events as conversions, or set up conversion tracking
with the Google tag. Add call tracking if phone calls are a big part of your business.

4. Set up the Meta pixel
Install the Meta pixel on your site and set up events for the same actions. The Conversions API,
which sends events from your server, makes tracking more reliable as browsers block more
cookies.

5. Tag every ad link with UTMs
Add UTM parameters to each ad's link so GA4 knows exactly where a visitor came from:
?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign=summer_offer

Use the same naming every time, or your reports become a mess.

6. Ask every new customer how they found you
Tracking misses things: people who see an ad on their phone and call later from a laptop, or hear
about you from a friend who saw it. A simple "how did you hear about us?" question fills the gaps.

7. Respect privacy choices
If your site shows a cookie banner, make sure your tracking follows the choices people make, and
that your privacy policy explains what you collect.

Your weekly 10-minute check
1. Spend this week, per platform
2. Enquiries this week, per platform
3. Cost per enquiry, compared with your target
4. Any ad or keyword spending money with no enquiries, paused
5. Enquiries from last week that became customers, logged
If you don't have a target cost per enquiry yet, read how much a small business should spend on
ads.
