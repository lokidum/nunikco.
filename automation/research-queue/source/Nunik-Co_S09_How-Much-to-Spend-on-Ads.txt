      How Much Should a Small Business
      Spend on Ads?
      A simple way to set an ad budget based on what a customer is worth, with a worked
      example and a 4-week test plan.
      By Lokesh Barani Kumar, founder of Nunik Co. · November 2026



A small business should set its ad budget by working backwards from what a new customer is
worth, not by picking a round number. Decide the most you'd pay to win one customer, estimate
how many enquiries it takes to win one, and that gives you a target cost per enquiry. Then commit
to a test budget you're comfortable spending for at least four weeks, so you have enough results to
make a real decision.

Step 1: Work out what a customer is worth
Start with the average amount a new customer spends with you, in their first job or over a typical
year. If customers come back, the yearly figure is the fairer one.

Step 2: Decide what you'd pay to win one
How much of that would you happily spend to win a new customer and still be glad you did? This is
your target cost per customer. Be honest about your margins.

Step 3: Work out your cost per enquiry
Not every enquiry becomes a customer. If you know roughly what share of enquiries you win,
divide your target cost per customer by the number of enquiries it takes.

A worked example
These numbers are illustrative. Swap in your own.

 Enquiries it takes to win one customer                                4

 Target cost per enquiry                                               $120 ÷ 4 = $30

If, after four weeks, enquiries are costing $30 or less and turning into customers at the rate you
expected, you can scale up. If they're costing far more, change one thing (the offer, the creative,
the landing page or the targeting) and test again before adding more money.

Your 4-week test plan
1. Before launch: set up conversion tracking so you can see every enquiry the ads produce. See
   how to tell if your ads are working.
2. Week 1: launch with two ad variations and a dedicated landing page. Don't touch anything for
   the first few days.
3. Week 2: check cost per enquiry. Pause anything clearly not working.
4. Weeks 3 and 4: let the stronger version run. Track which enquiries become customers.
5. End of week 4: compare actual cost per customer with your target, and decide: scale, change,
   or stop.

Common budget mistakes
    Spending too little to learn anything. A handful of clicks a week won't show you a pattern.
    Scaling before it works. More money on a weak campaign just loses money faster.
    Judging on clicks. Cheap clicks that never enquire are worth nothing.
    Forgetting the landing page. Most "the ads don't work" problems are really "the page doesn't
    convert" problems.
