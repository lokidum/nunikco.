01

The problem

Word of mouth is a great base for a driving school, but it's invisible to anyone searching "driving lessons near me". Rams needed to be found on Google, trusted on first look, and easy to contact.

02

What we did

Google Business Profile

We set up and verified the Google Business Profile, then built it out properly: services, service areas, photos and a profile image designed for a circular avatar. The school's horizontal logo would have cropped badly, so we made a square icon version.

Every review gets a personal reply written in the owner's own voice, not a template. New profiles go through a trust period with Google, so we kept the flow of reviews natural rather than chasing a sudden spike.

Website, three rounds

We didn't get it perfect the first time, and that's part of the story. Each rebuild fixed what we learned from the last one:

  • A memorable design. A scroll-driven road that draws itself down the page as visitors scroll, with smooth animations, so the site feels like a driving school rather than a template.
  • Suburb coverage. The suburbs the school serves are listed clearly, so it shows up for local searches and learners can see straight away whether they're covered.
  • Structured data. Schema markup tells Google and AI tools exactly what the business is, where it operates, and what it offers.
  • Open to AI crawlers. The site lets the bots used by ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and others read it, so the school can be recommended in AI answers.
  • WhatsApp contact. One tap to message the school directly.

04

What other local businesses can take from this

  1. Reviews compound. Each new review makes the next customer more likely to call. Replying personally shows future customers there's a real person behind the business.
  2. Your first website doesn't have to be your last. Launch, learn what customers respond to, and improve.
  3. Small technical details matter. Schema, suburb lists and AI crawler access take a day to set up and keep paying off.
  4. Measure paying customers, not just traffic. Clicks are easy to count. 5 new students a month is the number that pays the bills.
05

Frequently asked questions

How do driving instructors get more students online?

Start with a complete Google Business Profile and a steady flow of reviews, then a fast website that lists your suburbs, prices and services clearly and makes booking easy.

Do suburb pages help local SEO?

Yes, when each one is useful and specific. Listing the suburbs you serve helps Google match you to local searches. Avoid copy-paste pages that only swap the suburb name.

Should a small business reply to every Google review?

Yes. Replies show future customers you care, and a personal reply in the owner's voice reads far better than a template. Reply to negative reviews calmly and offer to sort it out offline.
