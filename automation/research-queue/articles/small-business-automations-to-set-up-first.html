The first automations a small business should set up are the ones that stop customers slipping through the cracks: an instant reply to enquiries, a lead alert to your phone, a follow-up on unaccepted quotes, appointment reminders, a review request after each job, a missed-call text back, and a check-in with past customers. Each one takes a few hours to set up and then runs on its own every day.

1. Instant enquiry reply

When someone fills in your form, they get an email or SMS straight away thanking them, confirming what they asked for, and saying when you'll be in touch. It buys you time and stops them moving on.

2. Lead alert to your phone

Every new enquiry gets pushed to your phone by SMS, WhatsApp or Telegram, with the key details. You see it on the job, not at 9pm.

3. Missed-call text back

If you can't answer, the caller gets a text: "Sorry we missed you, we're on a job. What can we help with?" Many people will reply by text when they wouldn't leave a voicemail. For more, an AI receptionist can answer the call itself.

4. Quote follow-up

If a quote hasn't been accepted after a few days, send a friendly check-in asking if they have any questions. Plenty of quotes go unanswered simply because people got busy.

5. Appointment reminders

A reminder the day before, with the time, address and anything they need to bring. One of the simplest ways to cut no-shows.

6. Review request

When a job is marked complete, the customer gets a thank-you with your Google review link. This is how review counts grow steadily without you having to remember.

7. Past customer check-in

A friendly message to past customers after a set time: a service reminder, a seasonal tip, or just checking in. Only send this with their consent, and include an unsubscribe option, as the Spam Act 2003 requires.
