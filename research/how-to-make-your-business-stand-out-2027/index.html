10 Ways to Make Your Business
Stand Out in 2027

Practical ways Australian small businesses can win more customers, from Google reviews to showing up in ChatGPT answers.

Published 20 November 2026 / by Lokesh Barani Kumar

To make your business stand out in 2027, you need three things working together: people can find you (Google Business Profile, reviews, a fast website), AI tools can understand and recommend you (clear answers, schema, consistent details), and customers remember you (a real brand, real faces, fast follow-up). Most local businesses do one of these well. Doing all three is where you pull ahead.

I run Nunik Co., a small web and automation studio, and most of what's below comes from building sites for local businesses like driving schools, food trucks and market stalls. Nothing here needs a big budget. A lot of it needs about an afternoon.

Quick facts

  • One driving school site we built gets about 150 clicks and more than 7,000 impressions a month from Google Search; another has passed 100 Google reviews.
  • One of them grew to over 100 Google reviews and turns about 5 website leads into paying students every month.
  • Both are small, owner-run businesses without a marketing team.

02

How do small businesses in Australia get more customers?

Most local customers start with a Google search, check your reviews, glance at your website and then decide whether to call. Tactics 1 to 3 cover that whole path. If any one of them is weak, people quietly pick someone else.

1. Own your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing people see, before your website. Claim it, verify it, and fill in every field: categories, service areas, hours, services with prices, photos, and a description that says plainly what you do and where.

One thing that catches people out: make sure you hold owner access. We've had a launch held up because a third party still controlled the profile. Check this before anything else.

Post an update or photo every week or two. It keeps the profile looking alive and gives Google more to work with. There's a full list in our Google Business Profile checklist.

2. Build a steady flow of real reviews

Reviews do two jobs. They help you rank in the map results, and they are the main thing people read before they call. Rams Driving Academy grew past 100 Google reviews, and the owner replies to reviews personally, in his own voice.

The word that matters is steady. A burst of 30 reviews in one week on a new profile can trigger Google's filters. A few every week looks like what it is: a real business with happy customers. Never offer discounts or gifts for reviews, and never filter so only happy customers get asked. Both break Google's rules. More on this in how to get more Google reviews.

3. A fast website with one clear call to action

Your website has one job: turn a visitor into an enquiry. That means it loads fast on a phone, says what you do in the first line, shows proof (reviews, photos, licences), and has one obvious next step. Book a lesson. Get a quote. Message us on WhatsApp.

I see a lot of small business sites with five buttons competing for attention. Pick one. For Adelaide Confident Driving Academy we made WhatsApp booking the single main action, because it's the quickest way for a nervous learner to ask a question. It sounds small, but it changed how the whole site was built.

03

How do you show up in ChatGPT and Google AI answers?

To show up in AI answers, your website needs clear, direct answers to real questions, structured data that labels who you are, and business details that match everywhere online. AI tools pull from pages they can read easily and trust. Tactics 4 to 6 make your site one of those pages. If the terms are new, start with what AEO and GEO mean.

4. Answer the questions customers actually ask

Write down the 10 questions you get asked most on the phone. How much does it cost? How long does it take? Do you service my suburb? Then answer each one on your website in plain words, with the answer in the first sentence.

This helps in three places at once. Google can show your answer directly in search, AI tools can quote it, and customers get what they need without ringing you. Put the main ones in an FAQ section on your home page and service pages.

5. Make your business easy for AI to read

Behind the scenes, a few technical pieces help a lot:

  • Schema markup: a small block of code that tells search engines your business name, address, hours, services and reviews in a format they understand without guessing.
  • An llms.txt file: a short plain-text summary of your business and key pages, written for AI tools.
  • Letting AI crawlers in: some website setups block the bots that ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity use. If they can't read your site, they can't recommend you.
  • Content that loads without JavaScript tricks, so every crawler sees the same page your customers do.

When we rebuilt the Rams Driving Academy site, we added all four, plus a page section for each suburb they cover. Your web developer should be able to do this in a day. If they don't know what you mean, that tells you something.

6. Keep your details identical everywhere

Your business name, address, phone number and hours should be exactly the same on your website, Google profile, Facebook, Instagram, Yellow Pages, True Local and anywhere else you're listed. Same spelling, same format.

It sounds boring. It matters because Google and AI tools cross-check these details to decide whether you're a real, trustworthy business. Mismatched phone numbers or an old address from three years ago create doubt. Spend an hour searching your business name and fixing what you find.

04

Small business branding ideas that actually work

Good branding for a small business is not a fancy logo. It's being recognisable and remembered, so that when someone needs what you sell, your name comes to mind first and the referral sounds confident. Tactics 7 to 10 are about that.

7. A brand people remember

Pick a clear name, two or three colours, one or two fonts, and use them everywhere: website, car signage, business cards, social posts, uniforms. Consistency does more than cleverness.

The best branding ideas often come from the business itself. Adelaide Confident Driving Academy colour-codes its two main tests to match real Australian plates: the learner test in yellow and black, the P-plate test in red and white. Students recognise the system straight away, across the website, the cards and the car magnets.

8. Show the people behind the business

People buy from people. A 30-second video of you explaining something useful, a photo of the team on a job, or a short story about why you started will do more for trust than any stock photo.

You don't need a studio. A phone, decent light and something worth saying is enough. Post it to Instagram, TikTok and Facebook, and put the best ones on your website.

9. Partner with other local businesses

Find businesses that serve the same customers but don't compete with you. A driving school and a car dealer. A food truck and a market organiser. A plumber and a real estate agent.

Refer work to each other, mention each other online, link to each other's websites. These links also help your search rankings, because they come from real, relevant local businesses.

10. Follow up automatically

Most lost customers aren't lost to a competitor. They're lost because nobody got back to them quickly enough. Set up simple automations: an instant reply when someone fills in your form, a reminder the day before an appointment, a review request after the job is done.

This is the tactic that ties everything else together. All the visibility in the world doesn't help if enquiries sit unanswered over the weekend. Our guide to lead generation and onboarding goes much deeper on this.

Start here

Where to start if you only have one afternoon

If this list feels like a lot, do these three things this week:

  1. Claim and fill in your Google Business Profile.
  2. Text your last 10 happy customers a link to leave a review.
  3. Write answers to your 5 most common questions and put them on your website.

That alone puts you ahead of most of your local competitors.

06

Frequently asked questions

How can a small business stand out from competitors in 2027?

Focus on being easy to find, easy to trust and easy to contact. A complete Google Business Profile, a steady flow of real reviews, a fast website with one clear action, and quick follow-up will beat a bigger competitor who does none of these well.

What is the fastest way to get more customers for a small business in Australia?

For most local service businesses, the fastest wins come from your Google Business Profile and reviews. They affect where you show on Google Maps, and they're free. A clearer website and faster replies to enquiries come next.

How do I get my business mentioned by ChatGPT?

Publish clear answers to common customer questions on your website, add schema markup, allow AI crawlers in your robots.txt file, and keep your business details consistent across every listing. AI tools recommend businesses they can read clearly and verify from several sources.

Do I need to pay for ads to stand out?

No. Both driving schools in our case studies built steady traffic and leads by getting the basics right first. Ads can speed things up once the basics work, but they can't fix a weak profile or a website that doesn't convert. See our guide on how to run ads that work.

How many Google reviews does a small business need?

There's no magic number, but you want more recent reviews than your nearest competitors and a rating above 4.5. Recent matters as much as total. A business with 40 reviews from this year often looks better than one with 200 from five years ago.

How long does it take to see results from these changes?

Google Business Profile and review improvements can show up within weeks. Website and search ranking changes usually take two to four months to build. AI tools can take longer, because they update what they know less often.

Can I do all of this myself?

Tactics 1, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 9 are very doable on your own. Tactics 3, 5 and 10 usually need a web developer or someone comfortable with automation tools.
